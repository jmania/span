# Span

## Idea

Span helps people understand the distance between themselves and others at an event: the people they already know, possible introductions, and people they would like to meet. Its name also carries a quiet San Francisco reference through the bridge span.

**Positioning:** a personal tool for turning an attendee list into useful context for meeting people.

**Tagline:** See who you know. Find who to meet.

**Creator:** Josh Jacobson — https://www.linkedin.com/in/josh--jacobson/

## Visual direction

The primary exploration is a minimal bridge: two upright towers, one connecting cable, and one deck. The orange mark on warm ivory should remain recognizable at app-icon sizes. An alternate exploration uses two pillars and one curved connection on petrol teal.

Use bold geometric shapes, generous spacing, and a restrained palette of vermilion, warm ivory, and deep petrol. Avoid realistic bridge scenes, elaborate cable meshes, tiny nodes, gradients, clay textures, globes, hands, tourist imagery, and conference merchandise aesthetics.

The supplied raster illustrations are concept explorations made with the built-in image-generation tool. The primary bridge concept is used in the preview app while the mark is reviewed; it can subsequently be redrawn as a precise vector system.

## Voice and product truth

Use concrete, understated language. Explain what helps the attendee make a decision. Show Josh’s credit quietly in the header and an optional invitation after the useful results.

The app compares a user's first-degree export locally. Second-degree classifications are reviewed by the user; do not imply that Span automatically retrieves mutual contacts or connection graphs. The bridge is a brand metaphor, not a claim of additional data access.

Span is an independent project. References to the event explain compatibility only; do not use Lenny branding, event logos, or wording that implies official affiliation.
